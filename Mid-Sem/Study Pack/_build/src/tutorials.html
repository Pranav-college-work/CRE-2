Q2 · External area of FCC zeolite-Y spheres (2 marks)

For a sphere of diameter \(d_p\) (radius \(R\)) and particle density \(\rho_p\):

$$S_v = \frac{\pi d_p^2}{\pi d_p^3/6} = \frac{6}{d_p} = \frac{3}{R},\qquad S_g = \frac{S_v}{\rho_p} = \frac{3}{\rho_pR}$$

Taking 100 \(\mu\mathrm{m}\) as the radius (matches the answer key)

$$S_v = \frac{3}{1\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}} = 3\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{m^2/m^3}$$ $$R = \frac{3}{\rho_pS_g} = \frac{3}{(1.5)(3.2\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{cm^2/g})} = 6.25\times10^{-7}\ \text{cm}$$

Taking 100 \(\mu\mathrm{m}\) as the diameter

$$S_v = \frac{6}{1\times10^{-4}} = 6\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{m^2/m^3},\qquad S_g = 0.04\ \mathrm{m^2/g}$$ $$d_p = \frac{6}{\rho_pS_g} = 1.25\times10^{-6}\ \text{cm}\ (12.5\ \text{nm})$$
Point of the question

External area is tiny (0.02–0.04 \(\mathrm{m^{2}/g}\)). The 300 \(\mathrm{m^{2}/g}\) of a real FCC catalyst can only come from internal pores; particles of 10 nm could not be made or fluidised.

T2Tutorial 2 — adsorption equilibria

Q1 · Dissociative chemisorption of \(\mathrm{N_{2}}\) and \(\mathrm{H_{2}}\) on Fe (2 marks)

Show \(C_{N\cdot S} = \dfrac{C_t\sqrt{K_{N_2}p_{N_2}}}{1 + 2\sqrt{K_{N_2}p_{N_2}} + 2\sqrt{K_{H_2}p_{H_2}}}\).

  1. Mechanism: \(\mathrm{N_2 + 2S \rightleftharpoons 2N\cdot S}\) and \(\mathrm{H_2 + 2S \rightleftharpoons 2H\cdot S}\).
  2. Net rates (elementary, pairs of adjacent sites): $$r_{N} = k_N\left(p_{N_2}C_v^2 - \frac{C_{N\cdot S}^2}{K_{N_2}}\right),\qquad r_H = k_H\left(p_{H_2}C_v^2 - \frac{C_{H\cdot S}^2}{K_{H_2}}\right)$$
  3. At equilibrium both are zero: $$C_{N\cdot S} = C_v\sqrt{K_{N_2}p_{N_2}},\qquad C_{H\cdot S} = C_v\sqrt{K_{H_2}p_{H_2}}$$
  4. Site balance, counting each dissociated molecule as tying up a pair of sites: $$C_t = C_v + 2C_{N\cdot S} + 2C_{H\cdot S} = C_v\left[1 + 2\sqrt{K_{N_2}p_{N_2}} + 2\sqrt{K_{H_2}p_{H_2}}\right]$$
  5. Substitute \(C_v\): $$\boxed{C_{N\cdot S} = \frac{C_t\sqrt{K_{N_2}p_{N_2}}}{1 + 2\sqrt{K_{N_2}p_{N_2}} + 2\sqrt{K_{H_2}p_{H_2}}}}\ \ \blacksquare$$
About the factor 2

Fogler's CO example (10-10, 10-11) gets a 2 because \(\mathrm{C\cdot S}\) and \(\mathrm{O\cdot S}\) both appear, each equal to \(\sqrt{KP}\,C_v\). If each atom is booked on its own site (\(C_t = C_v + C_{N\cdot S} + C_{H\cdot S}\)), the 2's disappear. The physics is the same: \(\sqrt{p}\) dependence, and hydrogen competes with nitrogen for sites.

Q3 · Tóth and multi-site Langmuir for \(\mathrm{CO_{2}}\) and \(\mathrm{CH_{4}}\) on zeolite 13X; heat of adsorption

Tóth (Eq. 2)

$$\theta = \frac{q}{q_{max}} = \frac{Kp}{\left[1+(Kp)^n\right]^{1/n}}$$

\(n\le1\) measures surface heterogeneity; \(n=1\) is Langmuir.

Multi-site Langmuir (Eq. 3)

$$\theta = \frac{q}{q_{max}} = Kp\left(1-\frac{q}{q_{max}}\right)^a$$

\(a\) = number of sites one molecule occupies; \(a=1\) is Langmuir. Implicit in \(q\), so regress in the pressure direction: \(p = \theta/[K(1-\theta)^a]\).

Strategy: fit all three temperatures together. \(q_{max}\), \(n\) and \(a\) belong to the adsorbent–adsorbate pair; all temperature dependence sits in \(K\). Multi-site \(q_{max}\) is fixed at the Tóth value, because \(q_{max}\) and \(a\) are strongly correlated.

T3Tutorial 3 — rate laws from mechanisms, and regression

Q1 · Irreversible surface-reaction-limited rate laws (Fogler Table 10-5)

Common steps: every adsorbing species is at adsorption equilibrium, \(C_{i\cdot S} = K_iP_iC_v\); site balance \(C_t = C_v + \sum C_{i\cdot S}\), so \(C_v = C_t/(1 + \sum K_iP_i)\).

(i) Single site \(A\cdot S \rightarrow B\cdot S\)

$$-r'_A = k_SC_{A\cdot S} = k_SK_AP_AC_v$$ $$C_t = C_v(1 + K_AP_A + K_BP_B)$$ $$-r'_A = \frac{kP_A}{1+K_AP_A+K_BP_B},\ \ k = k_SK_AC_t$$

(ii-a) Dual site \(A\cdot S + S \rightarrow B\cdot S + S\)

$$-r'_A = k_SC_{A\cdot S}C_v = k_SK_AP_AC_v^2$$ $$-r'_A = \frac{kP_A}{(1+K_AP_A+K_BP_B)^2},\ \ k = k_SK_AC_t^2$$

One \(C_v\) from the vacant neighbour, one inside \(C_{A\cdot S}\): the square.

(ii-b) Dual site \(A\cdot S + B\cdot S \rightarrow C\cdot S + S\)

$$-r'_A = k_SC_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S} = k_SK_AK_BP_AP_BC_v^2$$ $$C_t = C_v(1+K_AP_A+K_BP_B+K_CP_C)$$ $$-r'_A = \frac{kP_AP_B}{(1+K_AP_A+K_BP_B+K_CP_C)^2},\ \ k = k_SK_AK_BC_t^2$$

(iii) Eley–Rideal \(A\cdot S + B(g) \rightarrow C\cdot S\)

$$-r'_A = k_SC_{A\cdot S}P_B = k_SK_AP_AP_BC_v$$ $$C_t = C_v(1+K_AP_A+K_CP_C)$$ $$-r'_A = \frac{kP_AP_B}{1+K_AP_A+K_CP_C},\ \ k = k_SK_AC_t$$

B never touches a site, so it is absent from the site balance.

Live: what the four rate laws look like (initial rates, no products)

Each curve is scaled to its own maximum, so only the shape matters. For the two laws with a second reactant, \(P_B\) is held at 1 atm with \(K_B = K_A\). The Eley–Rideal curve has the single-site shape, because B from the gas only multiplies the rate.

Q2 · n-Pentene to i-pentene over alumina (Fogler Table 10-4)

Both mechanisms: adsorption equilibrium \(C_{N\cdot S} = K_NP_NC_v\); desorption equilibrium \(C_{I\cdot S} = K_IP_IC_v\); \(C_t = C_v(1+K_NP_N+K_IP_I)\); define \(K_P = K_NK_S/K_I\).

(i) Dual site: \(N\cdot S + S \rightleftharpoons I\cdot S + S\)

$$-r'_N = k_S\left(C_{N\cdot S}C_v - \frac{C_{I\cdot S}C_v}{K_S}\right) = k_SK_NC_v^2\left(P_N - \frac{K_IP_I}{K_NK_S}\right)$$ $$\boxed{-r'_N = \frac{k\,(P_N - P_I/K_P)}{(1+K_NP_N+K_IP_I)^2}},\quad k = k_SK_NC_t^2$$

(ii) Single site: \(N\cdot S \rightleftharpoons I\cdot S\)

$$-r'_N = k_S\left(C_{N\cdot S} - \frac{C_{I\cdot S}}{K_S}\right) = k_SK_NC_v\left(P_N - \frac{K_IP_I}{K_NK_S}\right)$$ $$\boxed{-r'_N = \frac{k\,(P_N - P_I/K_P)}{1+K_NP_N+K_IP_I}},\quad k = k_SK_NC_t$$

Check \(K_P\): at equilibrium the rate is zero, so \(K_P = (P_I/P_N)_{eq}\), the thermodynamic constant. ✔

Q3 · Fogler P10-3: t-butyl alcohol from isobutene and water

Statement (Fogler P10-3, p. 482)

t-Butyl alcohol (TBA), an octane enhancer used to replace lead additives in gasoline, is produced by the liquid-phase hydration (W) of isobutene (I) over an Amberlyst-15 catalyst [Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 27, 2224 (1988)]. The mechanism is believed to be \(I + S \rightleftharpoons I\cdot S\); \(W + S \rightleftharpoons W\cdot S\); \(W\cdot S + I\cdot S \rightleftharpoons TBA\cdot S + S\); \(TBA\cdot S \rightleftharpoons TBA + S\). Derive a rate law assuming (a) the surface reaction is rate-limiting; (b) the adsorption of isobutene is limiting; (c) Eley–Rideal kinetics, \(I\cdot S + W \rightarrow TBA\cdot S\), with the surface reaction limiting; (d) I and W adsorb on different sites, \(I + S_1 \rightleftharpoons I\cdot S_1\) and \(W + S_2 \rightleftharpoons W\cdot S_2\), TBA is not on the surface, and the surface reaction is limiting. (e) What generalisation can you make? Below, \(T\) stands for TBA, and concentrations \(C_i\) replace partial pressures in the liquid.

(a) Surface reaction limiting (dual site, same sites)

\(C_{I\cdot S} = K_IC_IC_v\), \(C_{W\cdot S} = K_WC_WC_v\), \(C_{T\cdot S} = K_TC_TC_v\).

$$r'_T = k_S\left(C_{I\cdot S}C_{W\cdot S} - \frac{C_{T\cdot S}C_v}{K_S}\right) = k_SK_IK_WC_v^2\left(C_IC_W - \frac{C_T}{K_c}\right)$$ $$r'_T = \frac{k\,(C_IC_W - C_T/K_c)}{(1 + K_IC_I + K_WC_W + K_TC_T)^2},\quad K_c = \frac{K_IK_WK_S}{K_T}$$

(b) Adsorption of isobutene limiting

\(r'_T = k_I(C_IC_v - C_{I\cdot S}/K_I)\). From the fast steps: \(C_{W\cdot S} = K_WC_WC_v\), \(C_{T\cdot S} = K_TC_TC_v\), and surface equilibrium \(C_{I\cdot S} = \dfrac{C_{T\cdot S}C_v}{K_SC_{W\cdot S}} = \dfrac{K_TC_T}{K_SK_WC_W}C_v\).

$$r'_T = \frac{k_IC_t\left(C_I - \dfrac{C_T}{K_cC_W}\right)}{1 + K_WC_W + K_TC_T + \dfrac{K_TC_T}{K_SK_WC_W}}$$

\(C_I\) is absent from the denominator (\(\mathrm{I\cdot S}\) is not at equilibrium with gas I).

(c) Eley–Rideal: \(I\cdot S + W \rightarrow T\cdot S\), surface reaction limiting

Water reacts straight from the liquid, so it is not in the site balance. With \(C_{I\cdot S} = K_IC_IC_v\) and \(C_{T\cdot S} = K_TC_TC_v\):

$$r'_T = k_SC_{I\cdot S}C_W,\qquad C_t = C_v(1 + K_IC_I + K_TC_T)$$ $$r'_T = \frac{k\,C_IC_W}{1 + K_IC_I + K_TC_T},\quad k = k_SK_IC_t$$

Taken as reversible, the numerator becomes \(k(C_IC_W - C_T/K_c)\) with \(K_c = K_IK_S/K_T\).

(d) I and W on different sites \(S_1\), \(S_2\); TBA not on the surface

\(I\cdot S_1 + W\cdot S_2 \rightleftharpoons TBA + S_1 + S_2\), surface reaction limiting. TBA leaves directly, so it is in neither site balance: \(C_{t1} = C_{v1}(1+K_IC_I)\) and \(C_{t2} = C_{v2}(1+K_WC_W)\).

$$r'_{TBA} = k_S\left(C_{I\cdot S_1}C_{W\cdot S_2} - \frac{C_{TBA}C_{v1}C_{v2}}{K_S}\right) = k_SK_IK_WC_{v1}C_{v2}\left(C_IC_W - \frac{C_{TBA}}{K_c}\right)$$ $$\boxed{r'_{TBA} = -r'_I = \frac{k\,[C_IC_W - C_{TBA}/K_c]}{(1 + K_WC_W)(1 + K_IC_I)}},\quad k = k_SK_IK_WC_{t1}C_{t2},\ K_c = K_IK_WK_S$$

This is the answer printed in Fogler.

(e) Generalisation. The numerator is always a kinetic constant times a driving force that vanishes at equilibrium. The denominator lists species in equilibrium with sites; its power equals the number of sites in the limiting step; species whose adsorption is limiting drop out of it; different site types give a product of separate brackets; species that never occupy a site (water in (c), TBA in (d)) are absent from the site balances.

Q4 · Fogler P10-5: ethylene hydrogenation on Co–Mo

Fogler P10-5 (p. 483): the rate law for the hydrogenation (H) of ethylene (E) to form ethane (A) over a cobalt–molybdenum catalyst [Collect. Czech. Chem. Commun. 51, 2760 (1988)] is \(-r'_E = \dfrac{kP_EP_H}{1 + K_EP_E}\). (a) Suggest a mechanism and rate-limiting step consistent with this rate law. (b) What was the most difficult part in finding the mechanism?

  1. Ethylene adsorbs molecularly: \(E + S \rightleftharpoons E\cdot S\) (equilibrium), \(C_{E\cdot S} = K_EP_EC_v\).
  2. Hydrogen reacts from the gas (Eley–Rideal): \(E\cdot S + H_2(g) \rightarrow A\cdot S\) — rate-limiting, \(-r'_E = k_SC_{E\cdot S}P_H\).
  3. Ethane desorbs: \(A\cdot S\rightleftharpoons A + S\), with ethane weakly adsorbed (\(K_AP_A\ll1\)).
  4. Site balance \(C_t = C_v(1 + K_EP_E)\), so $$-r'_E = \frac{k_SK_EC_t\,P_EP_H}{1 + K_EP_E} = \frac{kP_EP_H}{1+K_EP_E}\ ✔$$

Hydrogen appears in the numerator but not the denominator, the Eley–Rideal fingerprint (or hydrogen adsorbed so weakly that its coverage is negligible).

(b) The most difficult part is that the rate law cannot tell hydrogen reacting from the gas (Eley–Rideal) apart from hydrogen adsorbed so weakly (\(K_HP_H \ll 1\)) that it never shows in the denominator; both put \(P_H\) in the numerator only. The other judgement call is ethane: the rate law has no \(K_AP_A\) term, so ethane must be weakly adsorbed or desorb very fast.

Q5 · Fogler P10-6: propanol on platinum

Fogler P10-6 (p. 483), as printed: \(\mathrm{O_2 + 2S \rightleftharpoons 2O\cdot S}\); \(\mathrm{C_3H_6 + O\cdot S \rightarrow C_3H_5OH\cdot S}\); \(\mathrm{C_3H_5OH\cdot S \rightleftharpoons C_3H_5OH + S}\). Suggest a limiting step and derive the rate law.

  1. Choose the surface reaction (Eley–Rideal: gas propylene with adsorbed O) as limiting: \(-r'_{C_3H_6} = k_SP_{C_3H_6}C_{O\cdot S}\).
  2. Dissociative oxygen at equilibrium: \(C_{O\cdot S} = \sqrt{K_OP_{O_2}}\,C_v\).
  3. Product desorption at equilibrium: \(C_{P\cdot S} = K_PP_PC_v\) (P = propanol).
  4. Site balance \(C_t = C_v\left(1 + \sqrt{K_OP_{O_2}} + K_PP_P\right)\).
  5. $$-r'_{C_3H_6} = \frac{k\,P_{C_3H_6}\sqrt{P_{O_2}}}{1 + \sqrt{K_OP_{O_2}} + K_PP_P},\qquad k = k_SC_t\sqrt{K_O}$$

If the alcohol desorbs rapidly and is weakly adsorbed, drop \(K_PP_P\). The \(\sqrt{P_{O_2}}\) is the signature of dissociative oxygen.
