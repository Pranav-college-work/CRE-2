TExam traps

BET intercept

If the plotted axis does not start at zero, extrapolate the line to \(p/p_0 = 0\). And \(v_m = 1/(I+s)\), not \(1/I\).

Radius or diameter?

\(S_v = 6/d_p = 3/R\). Tutorial 1 Q2's key uses 100 \(\mu\mathrm{m}\) as radius. Say which you assume.

\(V_g\) per gram, porosity per volume

\(\varepsilon_p = V_g\rho_p\), never \(V_g\) alone. In bidisperse pellets, \(\varepsilon_\mu\) per pellet uses \(\rho_P\), per particle uses \(\rho_p\).

Site balance for dissociative adsorption

Count every adsorbed fragment: \(C_t = C_v + C_{C\cdot S} + C_{O\cdot S} = C_v(1 + 2\sqrt{KP})\).

Limiting species leaves the denominator

If adsorption of A limits, \(C_{A\cdot S}\) is not \(K_AP_AC_v\); it is found from the downstream equilibria, so \(K_AP_A\) does not appear.

Gas-phase species in site balances

Eley–Rideal reactants and non-adsorbing products (propylene, methane, \(\mathrm{H_{2}}\) in toluene) never enter the site balance.

Rate constant basis in \(M_T\)

Use the constant per pellet volume. \(k' \times\rho_c\) first. \(k''\) (per area) must be multiplied by \(S_a\rho_c\).

Levenspiel \(L\) vs Fogler \(R\)

Sphere: \(M_T\) uses \(R/3\); \(\phi_1\) uses \(R\). \(\phi_1 = 3M_T\). Strong limit \(M_T>4\) is \(\phi_1>12\).

\(p_{H_2}\) is not \(p_H^2\)

Subscript 2 is part of the species name.

Temperature and diffusion

Raising \(T\) raises \(k\) much more than \(D_e\) or \(k_m\): transport limits get worse, not better.

Module 1

P1 · BET · 8 marks Nitrogen adsorption at −195.8 °C on 1 g of a catalyst gave \(v\) = 53.1, 61.2, 66.8, 72.1, 77.7, 83.8 \(\mathrm{cm^{3}(STP)/g}\) at \(p/p_0\) = 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30. Find \(v_m\), \(c\), \(S_g\), and the Wheeler mean pore radius if \(V_g = 0.45\) \(\mathrm{cm^{3}/g}\).

Compute \(y = \dfrac{x}{v(1-x)}\) with \(x = p/p_0\) (since \(p/[v(p_0-p)] = x/[v(1-x)]\)):

\(x\)0.050.100.150.200.250.30
\(y\times10^3\) \(\mathrm{(g/cm^{3})}\)0.9911.8162.6423.4674.2905.114

Least squares: slope \(s = 16.49\times10^{-3}\), intercept \(I = 0.167\times10^{-3}\).

$$v_m = \frac{1}{(16.49+0.167)\times10^{-3}} = 60.0\ \mathrm{cm^3/g},\qquad c = \frac{16.49}{0.167}+1 \approx 100$$ $$S_g = 4.35\times10^4(60.0) = 2.61\times10^6\ \mathrm{cm^2/g} = 261\ \mathrm{m^2/g}$$ $$\bar a = \frac{2V_g}{S_g} = \frac{2(0.45)}{2.61\times10^6} = 3.45\times10^{-7}\ \text{cm} = 34.5\ \text{Å}$$

A mesoporous catalyst (diameter about 7 nm), provided the pores are monodisperse.

P2 · He–Hg · 5 marks A 50.0 g sample displaces 18.0 \(\mathrm{cm^{3}}\) of helium and 40.0 \(\mathrm{cm^{3}}\) of mercury. Packed in a bed, 50.0 g occupies 66.7 \(\mathrm{cm^{3}}\). Find \(V_g\), \(\rho_s\), \(\rho_p\), \(\varepsilon_p\), bed voidage and the fraction of bed volume that is pore.
$$V_g = \frac{40.0-18.0}{50.0} = 0.44\ \mathrm{cm^3/g},\quad \rho_s = \frac{50}{18} = 2.78,\quad \rho_p = \frac{50}{40} = 1.25\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$$ $$\varepsilon_p = V_g\rho_p = 0.55\quad\left(\text{check } \frac{0.44\times2.78}{0.44\times2.78+1} = 0.55\right)$$ $$\rho_B = \frac{50}{66.7} = 0.75,\quad \varepsilon_B = 1 - \frac{\rho_B}{\rho_p} = 1 - \frac{0.75}{1.25} = 0.40$$

Pore fraction of the bed \(= (1-\varepsilon_B)\varepsilon_p = 0.60\times0.55 = 0.33\); solid \(= 0.60\times0.45 = 0.27\); void 0.40. Close to the 30/30/40 rule.

P3 · Global rate · 5 marks First-order reaction on non-porous particles: \(k = 0.50\) cm/s (per unit surface), \(k_m = 2.0\) cm/s, \(a_m = 20\) \(\mathrm{cm^{2}/g}\), bulk \(C_b = 1.0\times10^{-5}\) \(\mathrm{mol/cm^{3}}\). Find the global rate, \(C_s\), the share of resistance due to mass transfer, and the error if film resistance were ignored.
$$r_p = \frac{a_mC_b}{1/k + 1/k_m} = \frac{20(1.0\times10^{-5})}{2.0 + 0.5} = 8.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{mol/(g\cdot s)}$$ $$C_s = \frac{k_m}{k_m+k}C_b = \frac{2.0}{2.5}(1.0\times10^{-5}) = 8.0\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{mol/cm^3}$$

Mass-transfer share of the resistance \(= (1/k_m)/(1/k + 1/k_m) = 0.5/2.5 = 20\,\%\). Ignoring it gives \(ka_mC_b = 1.0\times10^{-4}\), a 25 % overestimate. Mostly reaction-controlled.

P4 · Pore size · 4 marks (a) What mercury pressure fills pores of radius 50 Å? What radius at 1000 psi? (b) During \(\mathrm{N_{2}}\) desorption at −195.8 °C, which pores are still filled at \(p/p_0 = 0.80\)?

(a) \(p = 8.75\times10^5/50 = 17\,500\) psi (needs high-pressure equipment). At 1000 psi, \(a = 875\) Å.

(b) Kelvin with Halsey correction:

$$\delta = 7.34\left(\ln\frac{1}{0.8}\right)^{-1/3} = 7.34(0.2231)^{-1/3} = 12.1\ \text{Å},\qquad a - \delta = \frac{9.52}{\log_{10}(1/0.8)} = \frac{9.52}{0.0969} = 98.2\ \text{Å}$$

\(a = 110\) Å. Pores smaller than about 110 Å radius still hold condensate; larger pores have emptied (apart from their adsorbed film).

P5 · Isotherm test · 6 marks CO uptake on a metal: \(P\) (atm) = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8; \(C\) (mmol/g) = 0.1854, 0.2485, 0.300, 0.3515, 0.400, 0.4433, 0.480. Molecular or dissociative? Find \(C_t\) and \(K\).

Molecular test: \(P/C\) vs \(P\) gives \(R^2 = 0.998\), but the residuals curve systematically (open the live chart on the M2 page). Dissociative test: \(\sqrt P/C\) vs \(\sqrt P\):

\(\sqrt P\)0.3160.5000.7071.0001.4142.0002.828
\(\sqrt P/C\)1.7062.0122.3572.8453.5364.5125.893

Perfect straight line (\(R^2 = 1.0000\)): slope 1.667, intercept 1.179.

$$\text{slope} = \frac{2}{C_t}\Rightarrow C_t = 1.20\ \mathrm{mmol/g};\qquad \text{intercept} = \frac{1}{C_t\sqrt K}\Rightarrow \sqrt K = \frac{\text{slope}}{2\times\text{intercept}} = 0.707,\ K = 0.50\ \mathrm{atm^{-1}}$$

Lesson: a high \(R^2\) alone does not choose a model; look at the residual pattern.

P6 · Derivation · 8 marks \(A + B\rightleftharpoons C\) on a catalyst. Both A and B adsorb molecularly on the same sites; adsorbed A and B react on adjacent sites to adsorbed C, which desorbs. Derive the rate law for surface-reaction control and state the initial rate when \(P_B = P_{A0}\) is varied at fixed feed ratio 1:1.

Mechanism: \(A + S\rightleftharpoons A\cdot S\); \(B+S\rightleftharpoons B\cdot S\); \(A\cdot S + B\cdot S\rightleftharpoons C\cdot S + S\) (limiting); \(C\cdot S\rightleftharpoons C + S\).

$$-r'_A = k_S\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S} - \frac{C_{C\cdot S}C_v}{K_S}\right),\quad C_{i\cdot S} = K_iP_iC_v$$ $$-r'_A = k_SK_AK_BC_v^2\left(P_AP_B - \frac{K_CP_C}{K_AK_BK_S}\right),\quad C_v = \frac{C_t}{1 + K_AP_A + K_BP_B + K_CP_C}$$ $$\boxed{-r'_A = \frac{k\,(P_AP_B - P_C/K_P)}{(1 + K_AP_A + K_BP_B + K_CP_C)^2}},\quad k = k_SK_AK_BC_t^2,\ K_P = \frac{K_AK_BK_S}{K_C}$$

Initial, \(P_A = P_B = P_0\), \(P_C = 0\): \(-r'_{A0} = \dfrac{kP_0^2}{[1 + (K_A+K_B)P_0]^2}\). This rises as \(P_0^2\) at low pressure and levels off to \(k/(K_A+K_B)^2\). (If only \(P_A\) is raised at fixed \(P_B\), the rate passes through a maximum: A crowds B off the surface.)

P7 · Using a rate law · 4 marks For toluene hydrodemethylation (Fogler parameters) find the rate and the fractions of vacant, toluene-covered and benzene-covered sites at \(P_T = 4\), \(P_{H_2} = 10\), \(P_B = 3\) atm.
$$1 + K_BP_B + K_TP_T = 1 + 1.39(3) + 1.038(4) = 1 + 4.170 + 4.152 = 9.322$$ $$-r'_T = \frac{1.45\times10^{-8}(4)(10)}{9.322} = 6.22\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{mol/(g\text{-}cat\cdot s)}$$ $$\frac{C_v}{C_t} = \frac{1}{9.322} = 0.107,\quad \frac{C_{T\cdot S}}{C_t} = \frac{4.152}{9.322} = 0.445,\quad \frac{C_{B\cdot S}}{C_t} = \frac{4.170}{9.322} = 0.447$$

Benzene already occupies as many sites as toluene: product inhibition is strong.

P8 · Initial rates · 6 marks Initial rates for \(A\rightarrow B\): \(P_{A0}\) = 1, 4, 16 atm gives \(-r'_{A0}\) = 2.00, 4.00, 5.33 \(\mathrm{mol/(kg\cdot h)}\). Which step limits? Find the parameters.

Rate rises but less than proportionally and tends to a plateau: surface reaction limiting, single site, \(-r'_{A0} = kP_{A0}/(1+K_AP_{A0})\). Linearise:

$$\frac{P_{A0}}{-r'_{A0}} = \frac1k + \frac{K_A}{k}P_{A0}:\qquad (1, 0.50),\ (4, 1.00),\ (16, 3.00)$$

Slope \(= (3.00-0.50)/15 = 0.1667 = K_A/k\); intercept \(= 0.50 - 0.1667 = 0.3333 = 1/k\). So \(k = 3.0\) \(\mathrm{mol/(kg\cdot h\cdot atm)}\), \(K_A = 0.50\) \(\mathrm{atm^{-1}}\). Plateau \(k/K_A = 6\) \(\mathrm{mol/(kg\cdot h)}\).

P9 · \(\eta\) from properties · 5 marks First-order reaction in 6 mm diameter spheres: \(k''' = 2.0\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), \(\mathscr{D}_e = 5.0\times10^{-7}\) \(\mathrm{m^{2}/s}\), \(C_{As} = 10\) \(\mathrm{mol/m^{3}}\). Find the regime, \(\eta\) and the observed rate.
$$L = \frac R3 = \frac{0.003}{3} = 0.001\ \text{m},\qquad M_T = 0.001\sqrt{\frac{2.0}{5.0\times10^{-7}}} = 2.00$$ $$\eta = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\tanh6} - \frac16\right) = 0.5(1.0000 - 0.1667) = 0.417$$

Intermediate regime. \(-r'''_{A,obs} = \eta k'''C_{As} = 0.417(2.0)(10) = 8.33\) \(\mathrm{mol/(m^{3}\cdot s)}\). Fogler check: \(\phi_1 = 6\), \(\eta = \frac{3}{36}(6\coth6 - 1) = 0.417\).

P10 · Wagner modulus · 6 marks Spheres of 12 mm diameter show \(-r'''_{A,obs} = 3.0\) \(\mathrm{mol/(m^{3}\ cat\cdot s)}\) at \(C_{As} = 20\) \(\mathrm{mol/m^{3}}\); \(\mathscr{D}_e = 2.0\times10^{-6}\) \(\mathrm{m^{2}/s}\). First order. Is pore resistance significant? Find the intrinsic \(k'''\).
$$L = \frac{0.006}{3} = 0.002\ \text{m},\qquad M_W = \frac{L^2(-r'''_A)_{obs}}{\mathscr{D}_eC_{As}} = \frac{(0.002)^2(3.0)}{(2.0\times10^{-6})(20)} = 0.30$$

\(0.15 < M_W < 4\): intermediate. Solve \(M_T^2\eta(M_T) = 0.30\) for a sphere by trial: \(M_T = 0.60\) gives \(\eta = \frac{1}{0.6}\left(\frac{1}{\tanh1.8} - \frac{1}{1.8}\right) = 0.835\) and \(0.36\times0.835 = 0.30\). ✔

$$k''' = \frac{M_T^2\mathscr{D}_e}{L^2} = \frac{0.36(2.0\times10^{-6})}{4\times10^{-6}} = 0.180\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\qquad\left(\text{check: } 0.835\times0.180\times20 = 3.0\ ✔\right)$$

P12 · Falsified kinetics · 4 marks Large pellets give an apparent order 1.5 and apparent activation energy 65 kJ/mol. Crushed catalyst behaves differently. What are the true order and activation energy if \(E_{diff} = 10\) kJ/mol?

Strong pore resistance: \(n_{obs} = (n+1)/2 = 1.5\Rightarrow n = 2\). \(E_{obs} = (E_{true}+E_{diff})/2 \Rightarrow E_{true} = 2(65) - 10 = 120\) kJ/mol. The crushed (small) particles show the intrinsic values.

P13 · Effective diffusivity · 3 marks \(D_{AB} = 1.0\times10^{-5}\) \(\mathrm{m^{2}/s}\), \(\varphi_p = 0.45\), \(\sigma_c = 0.8\), \(\tilde\tau = 3.5\). Find \(D_e\) and explain each factor.
$$D_e = \frac{(1.0\times10^{-5})(0.45)(0.8)}{3.5} = 1.03\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2/s}\approx0.10\,D_{AB}$$

\(\varphi_p\): only the void fraction of the cross-section is open. \(\sigma_c\): varying pore cross-sections constrict flow. \(\tilde\tau\): the path is longer than the straight line.

P14 · Rate constants and \(\varphi\) · 5 marks \(k''_1 = 1.0\times10^{-9}\) m/s, \(S_a = 200\) \(\mathrm{m^{2}/g}\), \(\rho_c = 1.2\) \(\mathrm{g/cm^{3}}\), 4 mm diameter spheres, \(D_e = 1.0\times10^{-7}\) \(\mathrm{m^{2}/s}\). Find \(k'_1\), \(k_1\), \(\phi_1\) and \(\eta\).
$$k'_1 = k''_1S_a = (1.0\times10^{-9})(2.0\times10^5\ \mathrm{m^2/kg}) = 2.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^3/(kg\cdot s)}$$ $$k_1 = k'_1\rho_c = (2.0\times10^{-4})(1200) = 0.24\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$$ $$\phi_1 = R\sqrt{\frac{k_1}{D_e}} = 0.002\sqrt{\frac{0.24}{1.0\times10^{-7}}} = 3.10,\qquad \eta = \frac{3}{3.10^2}(3.10\coth3.10 - 1) = 0.660$$

R30 rapid-fire questions

1. What does a catalyst change and not change?

Changes rate (activation energy, mechanism) and selectivity; not \(\Delta H\), \(\Delta G\) or equilibrium.

2. Which steps disappear for a non-porous catalyst?

Steps 2 and 6 (internal diffusion).

3. Why is a non-catalytic gas–solid reaction inherently transient?

The solid reactant is consumed, so the geometry and rate change with time.

4. Name the three electronic classes of catalysts with an example each.

Conductors (Pt, Ni), semiconductors (NiO, ZnO), insulators/solid acids (silica–alumina).

5. Wet vs dry impregnation?

Wet: excess solution, diffusion. Dry (incipient wetness): exactly pore volume, diffusion plus capillary flow.

6. Why reduce Ni catalyst inside the reactor?

To avoid contact with air and other gases that would poison the reactive nickel.

7. Which poison type does coke represent?

Deposited (and a diffusion poison when it blocks pore mouths); partly reversible by burning.

8. Which Langmuir assumption do Temkin and Freundlich relax?

Constant heat of adsorption (uniform surface).

9. What is the physical meaning of the BET constant \(c\)?

\(c = \exp[(E_1 - E_L)/RT]\): how much more strongly the first layer is held than later (liquid-like) layers.

10. Why nitrogen at its boiling point for BET?

It physisorbs on any surface, builds multilayers, is cheap, and its molecular area (16.2 \(\mathrm{\text{Å}^{2}}\)) is well established.

11. Why does helium measure solid volume?

It enters all pores but not the solid, and hardly adsorbs.

12. What does high pelleting pressure do to macropores and micropores?

Squeezes out macropores (1.08 to 0.265 \(\mathrm{cm^{3}/g}\)) with little change to micropores or area.

13. Isotherm type with a hysteresis loop?

Type IV (and V): capillary condensation in mesopores.

14. Units of \(K_A\) (adsorption) and \(K_S\) (single site)?

\(K_A\): 1/atm. \(K_S\): dimensionless (single and dual site); 1/atm for Eley–Rideal.

15. How does \(K_A\) vary with temperature, and why?

Decreases: adsorption is exothermic; \(k_{-A}\) grows faster with \(T\) than \(k_A\).

16. Initial rate independent of pressure — which step limits?

Desorption.

17. Why is propylene absent from the cumene site balance?

It is not adsorbed; it leaves directly to the gas in the surface reaction.

18. What fraction of heterogeneous reactions are surface-reaction-limited?

More than 75 % (Fogler: 75–80 %).

19. What does an adsorbing inert do to the rate law?

Adds \(K_IP_I\) to the denominator, lowering the rate.

20. Why can a rate law fit never prove a mechanism?

Several mechanisms can give statistically indistinguishable fits; spectroscopy is needed. A misfit can disprove one.

21. Why does a fluidised bed use the CSTR equation?

The particles are well mixed, so the whole bed sees the exit composition; pressure drop is negligible.

22. Levenspiel's five factors?

Surface kinetics, pore diffusion, particle \(\Delta T\), film \(\Delta T\), film diffusion.

23. Why does Levenspiel prefer first-order kinetics for design?

Mechanisms are hard to prove and multi-constant forms cannot easily be combined with diffusion resistances; simple forms fit most data.

24. Boundary condition at the sealed end of a pore?

No flux: \(dC_A/dx = 0\) at \(x = L\).

25. Characteristic length of a 6 mm diameter cylinder (long)?

\(L = R/2 = 1.5\) mm.

26. \(\eta = 0.1\) for spheres. Levenspiel \(M_T\)? Fogler \(\phi_1\)?

Strong resistance: \(M_T\approx10\), \(\phi_1 = 3M_T\approx30\).

27. What happens to the Arrhenius plot when pore diffusion takes over?

The slope roughly halves: \(E_{obs}\approx E_{true}/2\).

28. Zero-order reaction under strong pore resistance appears as?

Half order.

29. In the Fogler shell balance, why does the flux come out negative?

Flux is defined positive outward (increasing \(r\)) while A diffuses inward.

30. Where should Pt be placed in a pellet with a very large Thiele modulus?

Only in a thin outer shell: the interior never sees reactant.
