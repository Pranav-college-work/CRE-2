Adsorption, surface reaction, desorption and catalytic rate laws

How to postulate a mechanism, pick a rate-limiting step and turn it into a rate law written only in measurable partial pressures; then how to get the parameters from data and design a reactor with them.

1Where we are heading

Rate laws in heterogeneous catalysis seldom follow power laws. Fogler teaches them "in reverse": first postulate mechanisms and derive the rate laws they imply, so that when you see data you recognise the form. In industry the order is the one in the figure.

2Steps 1 and 2 in brief (HSF 10.2.1, 10.2.2)

External diffusion (step 1)

$$\text{Rate} = k_C\,(C_{Ab} - C_{As}),\qquad k_C = \frac{D_{AB}}{\delta}$$

At low velocity the boundary layer \(\delta\) is thick and transport is slow. As \(U\) rises or \(D_p\) falls, \(k_C\) rises until a plateau where \(C_{Ab}\approx C_{As}\) and another step controls.

Internal diffusion (step 2)

$$\text{Rate} = k_r\,C_{As},\qquad k_r \propto \frac{1}{D_p}\ \text{(large pellets)}$$

In big pellets reactant is used up near the outside and the centre is wasted. Internal diffusion is not affected by the external flow. Small pellets: the surface steps control. Full treatment: Module 3.

3Adsorption isotherms from a site balance (HSF 10.2.3)

Notation

SymbolMeaningUnits
\(S\)a vacant active site—
\(A\cdot S\)one unit of A chemisorbed on a site—
\(C_t\)total concentration of active sites (constant unless the catalyst decays); order \(10^{22}\) sites/g-catmol/g-cat
\(C_v\)concentration of vacant sitesmol/g-cat
\(C_{i\cdot S}\)concentration of sites occupied by species \(i\)mol/g-cat
\(P_i\)partial pressure of \(i\) in the gas (by convention, instead of \(C_i\); \(P_i = C_iRT\))atm or kPa
10-1, site balance$$C_t = C_v + C_{A\cdot S} + C_{B\cdot S}$$

Molecular (non-dissociative) adsorption: \(\mathrm{CO + S \rightleftharpoons CO\cdot S}\)

Treat adsorption as an elementary step. Rate of attachment \(\propto\) collisions with vacant sites \(\propto P_{CO}C_v\); rate of detachment \(\propto C_{CO\cdot S}\):

10-3, 10-4$$r_{AD} = k_A P_{CO}C_v - k_{-A}C_{CO\cdot S} = k_A\left(P_{CO}C_v - \frac{C_{CO\cdot S}}{K_A}\right),\qquad K_A = \frac{k_A}{k_{-A}}$$

Units: \(r_{AD}\) \(\mathrm{mol/(\text{g-cat}\cdot s)}\), \(k_A\) \(\mathrm{1/(atm\cdot s)}\), \(K_A\) 1/atm. At equilibrium \(r_{AD}=0\):

10-6, 10-5, 10-7$$C_{CO\cdot S} = K_A P_{CO}C_v,\qquad C_t = C_v + C_{CO\cdot S}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \boxed{C_{CO\cdot S} = \frac{K_A P_{CO}\,C_t}{1 + K_A P_{CO}}}$$
10-8, linear test$$\frac{P_{CO}}{C_{CO\cdot S}} = \frac{1}{K_A C_t} + \frac{P_{CO}}{C_t}\qquad\text{plot } P_{CO}/C_{CO\cdot S} \text{ vs } P_{CO}:\ \text{slope } 1/C_t$$

Temperature: \(k_A\) for molecular adsorption is almost independent of \(T\); \(k_{-A}\) rises exponentially, so \(K_A\) falls exponentially with increasing temperature.

Dissociative adsorption: \(\mathrm{CO + 2S \rightleftharpoons C\cdot S + O\cdot S}\)

A molecule that dissociates needs two adjacent vacant sites. The probability of a vacant pair is \(\propto C_v^2\); desorption needs two adjacent occupied sites:

10-9$$r_{AD} = k_A P_{CO}C_v^2 - k_{-A}C_{C\cdot S}C_{O\cdot S} = k_A\left(P_{CO}C_v^2 - \frac{C_{C\cdot S}\,C_{O\cdot S}}{K_A}\right)$$

At equilibrium with \(C_{C\cdot S} = C_{O\cdot S}\):

10-10, 10-11$$C_{O\cdot S} = (K_A P_{CO})^{1/2}C_v,\qquad C_t = C_v + C_{O\cdot S} + C_{C\cdot S} = C_v\left[1 + 2(K_A P_{CO})^{1/2}\right]\ \Rightarrow\ \boxed{C_{O\cdot S} = \frac{(K_A P_{CO})^{1/2}\,C_t}{1 + 2(K_A P_{CO})^{1/2}}}$$
10-12, linear test$$\frac{P_{CO}^{1/2}}{C_{O\cdot S}} = \frac{1}{C_t K_A^{1/2}} + \frac{2\,P_{CO}^{1/2}}{C_t}\qquad\text{plot } P_{CO}^{1/2}/C_{O\cdot S} \text{ vs } P_{CO}^{1/2}:\ \text{slope } 2/C_t$$
Fingerprint

A square root of a partial pressure in a rate law means dissociative adsorption. Both \(k_A\) and \(k_{-A}\) increase exponentially with \(T\) here, but \(K_A\) still decreases.

4Surface reaction (HSF 10.2.4)

Once adsorbed, A can react in three kinds of ways. Each step is written as an elementary reaction, with surface concentrations for adsorbed species and partial pressures for gas-phase species.

MechanismElementary rate law\(K_S\) units
Single site \(A\cdot S \rightleftharpoons B\cdot S\)\(r_S = k_S\left(C_{A\cdot S} - \dfrac{C_{B\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)\) (10-15)dimensionless; \(k_S\) in 1/s
Dual site \(A\cdot S + S \rightleftharpoons B\cdot S + S\)\(r_S = k_S\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_v - \dfrac{C_{B\cdot S}C_v}{K_S}\right)\) (10-16)dimensionless; \(k_S\) in \(\mathrm{\text{g-cat}/(mol\cdot s)}\)
Dual site \(A\cdot S + B\cdot S \rightleftharpoons C\cdot S + D\cdot S\)\(r_S = k_S\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S} - \dfrac{C_{C\cdot S}C_{D\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)\) (10-17)dimensionless
Dual site, two site types \(A\cdot S + B\cdot S' \rightleftharpoons C\cdot S' + D\cdot S\)\(r_S = k_S\left(C_{A\cdot S}C_{B\cdot S'} - \dfrac{C_{C\cdot S'}C_{D\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)\) (10-18)dimensionless
Eley–Rideal \(A\cdot S + B(g) \rightleftharpoons C\cdot S\)\(r_S = k_S\left(C_{A\cdot S}P_B - \dfrac{C_{C\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)\) (10-19)1/atm; \(k_S\) in \(\mathrm{1/(atm\cdot s)}\)

5Desorption (HSF 10.2.5)

Desorption of C, \(C\cdot S \rightleftharpoons C + S\), is simply adsorption of C read right to left:

10-20, 10-21$$r_{DC} = k_D\left(C_{C\cdot S} - \frac{P_C C_v}{K_{DC}}\right),\qquad K_{DC} = \frac{1}{K_C}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ r_{DC} = k_D\left(C_{C\cdot S} - K_C P_C C_v\right),\qquad r_{DC} = -r_{ADC}$$

\(K_{DC}\) has units of atm; \(k_D\) of 1/s. The adsorption equilibrium constant of a species is the reciprocal of its desorption equilibrium constant.

6The rate-limiting step (HSF 10.2.6)

At steady state the three steps in series run at the same rate (there is no accumulation on the surface):

10-30$$-r'_A = r_{AD} = r_S = r_D$$

Usually one step is much slower. Think of an electric circuit: current \(I\) = rate, voltage \(E\) = driving force, resistances in series.

The Langmuir–Hinshelwood (Hougen–Watson) algorithm
  1. Postulate a mechanism: molecular or dissociative adsorption, single or dual site, Eley–Rideal. Write each step as reversible and elementary.
  2. Assume one step is rate-limiting. Its rate is the overall rate.
  3. For every other step, \(r/k \approx 0\) (its rate constant is huge), so it is at quasi-equilibrium. Use these to express every \(C_{i\cdot S}\) in terms of partial pressures and \(C_v\).
  4. Write the site balance and solve for \(C_v\).
  5. Substitute into the rate of the limiting step. Lump constants into \(k\). Replace ratios of constants by \(K_P\).
  6. Compare with data (initial-rate curves, runs with products added). If it disagrees, pick another limiting step; if all fail, another mechanism.
Why \(r/k\approx 0\) for the fast steps

All three rates are equal. If \(k_S\) and \(k_D\) are very large, then \(r_S/k_S\) and \(r_D/k_D\) are tiny while \(r_{AD}/k_A\) is not. Setting \(r_S/k_S = 0\) gives the equilibrium relation of the surface step. (Strictly, one should compare \(k_AP_C\) with \(k_S\) and \(k_D\) so that units match; the result is the same.)

Industrial adsorption-limited example

Ammonia synthesis on iron: \(\mathrm{H_2 + 2S \rightleftharpoons 2H\cdot S}\) (rapid); \(\mathrm{N_2 + 2S \rightleftharpoons 2N\cdot S}\) (rate-limiting: dissociative adsorption of \(\mathrm{N_2}\)); successive hydrogenations \(\mathrm{N\cdot S \to NH\cdot S \to H_2N\cdot S \to NH_3\cdot S}\) and desorption of \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) (rapid).

Industrial surface-reaction-limited example

\(\mathrm{CO + NO \rightarrow CO_2 + \tfrac12 N_2}\) over copper: adsorption of CO and NO rapid; \(\mathrm{NO\cdot S + CO\cdot S \rightleftharpoons CO_2 + N\cdot S + S}\) rate-limiting; \(\mathrm{N\cdot S + N\cdot S \rightleftharpoons N_2\cdot S + S}\) and \(\mathrm{N_2}\) desorption rapid. \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) and \(\mathrm{N_2}\) are weakly adsorbed.

General form (class notes p. 59; Levenspiel eq. 18.1) $$\text{rate} = \frac{(\text{kinetic term})\times(\text{driving force})}{(\text{resistance term})^n},\qquad\text{e.g.}\quad -r_A = \frac{k\,(p_A - p_B/K)}{1 + K_Ap_A + K_Bp_B}$$

The resistance term (denominator) lists the adsorbed species; the power \(n\) is the number of sites in the limiting step.

7Synthesising a rate law: cumene decomposition (HSF 10.3)

\(\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \rightleftharpoons C_6H_6 + C_3H_6}\) on Pt, written \(C \rightleftharpoons B + P\) (cumene, benzene, propylene). Mechanism (Table 10-3):

10-22 to 10-24$$\begin{aligned} &\text{Adsorption:} && C + S \rightleftharpoons C\cdot S && r_{AD} = k_A\left(P_CC_v - \frac{C_{C\cdot S}}{K_C}\right)\\ &\text{Surface reaction:} && C\cdot S \rightleftharpoons B\cdot S + P(g) && r_S = k_S\left(C_{C\cdot S} - \frac{P_P\,C_{B\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)\\ &\text{Desorption:} && B\cdot S \rightleftharpoons B + S && r_D = k_D\left(C_{B\cdot S} - K_BP_BC_v\right) \end{aligned}$$

Propylene is not adsorbed (\(C_{P\cdot S}=0\)). Forward: single site; reverse: Eley–Rideal (gas propylene meets adsorbed benzene). Units: \(k_A\) \(\mathrm{kPa^{-1}s^{-1}}\), \(K_C\) \(\mathrm{kPa^{-1}}\), \(k_S\) \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), \(K_S\) kPa, \(k_D\) \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\).

Case I · Is adsorption of cumene rate-limiting? (\(k_A \ll k_S, k_D\))

  1. Rate: \(-r'_C = r_{AD} = k_A\left(P_CC_v - C_{C\cdot S}/K_C\right)\). Need \(C_{C\cdot S}\) and \(C_v\).
  2. Surface reaction at equilibrium, \(r_S/k_S\approx 0\): \(C_{C\cdot S} = \dfrac{P_P\,C_{B\cdot S}}{K_S}\).
  3. Desorption at equilibrium, \(r_D/k_D\approx 0\): \(C_{B\cdot S} = K_BP_BC_v\). So \(C_{C\cdot S} = \dfrac{K_BP_BP_P}{K_S}C_v\).
  4. Substitute: $$r_{AD} = k_A\left(P_C - \frac{K_BP_BP_P}{K_SK_C}\right)C_v = k_A\left(P_C - \frac{P_BP_P}{K_P}\right)C_v,\qquad K_P = \frac{K_SK_C}{K_B}$$
  5. Site balance: \(C_t = C_v + C_{C\cdot S} + C_{B\cdot S} = C_v\left(1 + \dfrac{K_BP_BP_P}{K_S} + K_BP_B\right)\).
  6. Result: $$\boxed{-r'_C = \frac{C_tk_A\,(P_C - P_PP_B/K_P)}{1 + K_BP_PP_B/K_S + K_BP_B}}\quad(10\text{-}42)\qquad\text{initial: } -r'_{C0} = C_tk_AP_{C0} = kP_{C0}\ \ (10\text{-}43)$$

Initial rate (no products) would be linear in \(P_{C0}\) at all pressures.

Why \(K_P\) appears (10-38, 10-39)

At equilibrium \(r_{AD}=0\) gives \(P_{Ce} = P_{Be}P_{Pe}/(K_SK_C/K_B)\). Thermodynamics says \(K_P = P_{Be}P_{Pe}/P_{Ce}\) for \(C\rightleftharpoons B+P\). So \(K_P = K_CK_S/K_B\), and it can be found from \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\). Every limiting case contains the same driving force \(P_C - P_BP_P/K_P\), which vanishes at equilibrium, as it must.

Case II · Is the surface reaction rate-limiting? (\(k_S \ll k_A, k_D\))

  1. Rate: \(-r'_C = r_S = k_S\left(C_{C\cdot S} - P_PC_{B\cdot S}/K_S\right)\).
  2. Adsorption at equilibrium: \(C_{C\cdot S} = K_CP_CC_v\). Desorption at equilibrium: \(C_{B\cdot S} = K_BP_BC_v\).
  3. Substitute: $$r_S = k_S\left(K_CP_C - \frac{K_BP_BP_P}{K_S}\right)C_v = k_SK_C\left(P_C - \frac{P_BP_P}{K_P}\right)C_v\quad(10\text{-}26a)$$
  4. Site balance: \(C_t = C_v\,(1 + K_BP_B + K_CP_C)\).
  5. Result: $$\boxed{-r'_C = \frac{k\,(P_C - P_PP_B/K_P)}{1 + K_BP_B + K_CP_C}},\quad k = k_SC_tK_C\quad(10\text{-}44)\qquad -r'_{C0} = \frac{kP_{C0}}{1 + K_CP_{C0}}\quad(10\text{-}45)$$

Low \(P_{C0}\) (\(1\gg K_CP_{C0}\)): \(-r'_{C0}\approx kP_{C0}\), linear. High \(P_{C0}\) (\(K_CP_{C0}\gg 1\)): \(-r'_{C0}\approx k/K_C\), independent of pressure — the surface is saturated.

Case III · Is desorption of benzene rate-limiting? (\(k_D \ll k_A, k_S\))

  1. Rate: \(-r'_C = r_D = k_D\left(C_{B\cdot S} - K_BP_BC_v\right)\).
  2. Surface reaction at equilibrium: \(C_{B\cdot S} = \dfrac{K_SC_{C\cdot S}}{P_P}\). Adsorption at equilibrium: \(C_{C\cdot S} = K_CP_CC_v\). So \(C_{B\cdot S} = \dfrac{K_CK_SP_C}{P_P}C_v\) (10-47).
  3. Substitute: $$r_D = k_DK_CK_S\left(\frac{P_C}{P_P} - \frac{P_B}{K_P}\right)C_v\quad(10\text{-}48)$$
  4. Site balance: \(C_t = C_v\left(1 + K_CK_SP_C/P_P + K_CP_C\right)\) (10-49).
  5. Multiply numerator and denominator by \(P_P\): $$\boxed{-r'_C = \frac{k_DC_tK_SK_C\,(P_C - P_BP_P/K_P)}{P_P + P_CK_CK_S + K_CP_PP_C}}\quad(10\text{-}50)\qquad -r'_{C0} = k_DC_t$$

Initial rate would be independent of \(P_{C0}\): the surface is always full of benzene waiting to leave.

Which one fits? (HSF 10.3.4)

Live: initial rate vs initial cumene pressure for each limiting step

Adsorption limiting: \(-r'_{C0} = kP_{C0}\). Surface reaction limiting: \(-r'_{C0} = kP_{C0}/(1+K_CP_{C0})\). Desorption limiting: \(-r'_{C0} = k_DC_t\). Shapes, not magnitudes, identify the step. The experimental curve for cumene (Fogler Fig. 10-18) rises and levels off, so the surface reaction is rate-limiting.

Adsorbing inerts (10-51 to 10-53)

An inert I that adsorbs, \(I + S\rightleftharpoons I\cdot S\), with \(C_{I\cdot S} = K_IP_IC_v\), just adds a term to the site balance: \(-r'_C = \dfrac{k(P_C - P_BP_P/K_P)}{1 + K_CP_C + K_BP_B + K_IP_I}\). The rate falls as \(P_I\) rises, even though I does not react.

Class notes check

The notes (pp. 60–72) derive exactly these three cases, write the final form as \(-r'_C = \dfrac{k(P_C - P_BP_P/K_P)}{1 + K_BP_B + K_CP_C}\), and label the numerator as the kinetic term times the driving force, and the denominator as the resistance term.

8Reforming catalysts and Table 10-4 (HSF 10.3.5)

Octane is raised by turning straight chains into compact molecules (n-pentane octane number 62, i-pentane 90). On bifunctional Pt/\(\mathrm{Al_2O_3}\) (0.75 wt % Pt, Pt clusters about 10 Å on alumina pores 100–10 000 Å):

$$\text{n-pentane}\ \underset{\mathrm{Pt}}{\overset{-\mathrm{H_2}}{\rightleftharpoons}}\ \text{n-pentene}\ \underset{\mathrm{Al_2O_3}}{\rightleftharpoons}\ \text{i-pentene}\ \underset{\mathrm{Pt}}{\overset{+\mathrm{H_2}}{\rightleftharpoons}}\ \text{i-pentane}$$

Focus on the isomerisation \(N \rightleftharpoons I\) on alumina. Table 10-4 applies the algorithm.

Try 1: dual-site mechanism

\(N + S\rightleftharpoons N\cdot S\); \(N\cdot S + S \rightleftharpoons I\cdot S + S\); \(I\cdot S \rightleftharpoons I + S\).

$$-r'_N = r_S = k_S\left(C_vC_{N\cdot S} - \frac{C_{I\cdot S}C_v}{K_S}\right)$$ $$C_{N\cdot S} = K_NP_NC_v,\quad C_{I\cdot S} = K_IP_IC_v,\quad C_v = \frac{C_t}{1 + K_NP_N + K_IP_I}$$ $$-r'_N = \frac{k\,(P_N - P_I/K_P)}{(1 + K_NP_N + K_IP_I)^2},\quad k = k_SC_t^2K_N,\ K_P = \frac{K_NK_S}{K_I}$$

Try 2: single-site mechanism (the one that fits)

\(N + S\rightleftharpoons N\cdot S\); \(N\cdot S \rightleftharpoons I\cdot S\); \(I\cdot S \rightleftharpoons I + S\).

$$-r'_N = r_S = k_S\left(C_{N\cdot S} - \frac{C_{I\cdot S}}{K_S}\right)$$ $$= k_SK_N\left(P_N - \frac{P_I}{K_P}\right)C_v$$ $$-r'_N = \frac{k\,(P_N - P_I/K_P)}{1 + K_NP_N + K_IP_I},\quad k = k_SC_tK_N$$

These are Tutorial 3 Q2 (i) and (ii). Step 2 of the algorithm says start with the surface reaction: more than 75 % of heterogeneous reactions that are not diffusion-limited are surface-reaction-limited. If two models fit, discriminate statistically (residuals). A fit never proves a mechanism; spectroscopy is needed for that, but a misfit does disprove one.

10PSSH and temperature dependence (HSF 10.3.6, 10.3.7)

Pseudo-steady-state hypothesis

Instead of setting \(r/k\approx 0\) for the fast steps, treat each adsorbed species as a reactive intermediate whose net rate of formation is zero:

$$r^*_{i\cdot S} = 0$$

Used mainly when more than one step is rate-limiting. (Class notes p. 74: PSSH gives \(C_{T\cdot S}\approx C_{B\cdot S}\) roughly constant, \(r_{T\cdot S}\approx r_{B\cdot S}\approx 0\).)

High temperature

\(k\) follows Arrhenius and rises with \(T\). Adsorption is exothermic, so every \(K_i\) falls with \(T\): coverage drops and the denominator tends to 1.

$$-r'_A = \frac{kP_A}{1 + K_AP_A + K_BP_B}\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{high }T\ }\ -r'_A \approx kP_A$$

or \(-r'_A \approx k(P_A - P_B/K_P)\) if reversible. This is why Levenspiel (Module 3) is happy with first-order kinetics.

10.4.1 Deducing the rate law from the data

  1. Reversibility. Runs 3 (\(P_M=0\)) and 5 (\(P_M=1\)) give 41.6 and 42.0: the reverse reaction does not matter. Treat as irreversible.
  2. Methane. Runs 1 and 2: quadrupling \(P_M\) leaves the rate unchanged, \(71.0 \to 71.3\). Methane is not adsorbed (or \(K_MP_M\ll 1\)); it goes straight to the gas, like propylene for cumene.
  3. Benzene. Runs 3, 4, 6 (and 7 with \(P_B=0\)): rate falls \(71.8 \to 41.6 \to 19.7 \to 17.1\) as \(P_B\) goes \(0 \to 1 \to 4 \to 5\) atm. Benzene is in the denominator: it adsorbs. \(-r'_T\sim 1/(1 + K_BP_B + \dots)\). "If it is in the denominator, it is probably on the surface."
  4. Toluene. Runs 10–15: rate rises with \(P_T\) at low pressure (\(47 \to 71.3\)) and levels off at high pressure (\(131 \to 133\)). \(-r'_T \sim P_T/(1 + K_TP_T + \dots)\).
  5. Hydrogen. Runs 7–9: \(71.8 \to 142 \to 284\) when \(P_{H_2}\) goes \(1 \to 2 \to 4\) atm. First order, so \(\mathrm{H_2}\) is not adsorbed (or \(K_{H_2}P_{H_2}\ll1\)). If it were adsorbed it would behave like toluene.
10-71$$-r'_T = \frac{k\,P_{H_2}P_T}{1 + K_BP_B + K_TP_T}$$

10.4.2 A mechanism consistent with the observations

Eley–Rideal: toluene adsorbs, reacts with gas-phase hydrogen to give adsorbed benzene and gas methane; benzene desorbs. Assume the surface reaction is limiting (as it is for 75–80 % of mechanisms).

10-72 to 10-74$$\begin{aligned} &T(g) + S \rightleftharpoons T\cdot S && r_{AD} = k_A\left(C_vP_T - \frac{C_{T\cdot S}}{K_T}\right)\\ &H_2(g) + T\cdot S \rightleftharpoons B\cdot S + M(g) && r_S = k_S\left(P_{H_2}C_{T\cdot S} - \frac{C_{B\cdot S}P_M}{K_S}\right)\\ &B\cdot S \rightleftharpoons B(g) + S && r_D = k_D\left(C_{B\cdot S} - K_BP_BC_v\right) \end{aligned}$$

\(r_{AD}/k_A\approx 0\): \(C_{T\cdot S} = K_TP_TC_v\). \(r_D/k_D\approx0\): \(C_{B\cdot S} = K_BP_BC_v\). Site balance \(C_t = C_v + C_{T\cdot S} + C_{B\cdot S}\) gives \(C_v = C_t/(1 + K_TP_T + K_BP_B)\). Then

10-79, 10-80$$-r'_T = \frac{C_tk_SK_T\,(P_{H_2}P_T - P_BP_M/K_P)}{1 + K_TP_T + K_BP_B}\ \xrightarrow{\ K_P\ \text{very large}\ }\ \boxed{-r'_T = \frac{k\,P_{H_2}P_T}{1 + K_BP_B + K_TP_T}}$$

Papp et al. tested over 25 models; this one was the best.

10.4.3 Evaluating the parameters

Linearised form (10-81), useful for first guesses: divide both sides by \(P_{H_2}P_T\) and invert.

$$\frac{P_{H_2}P_T}{-r'_T} = \frac{1}{k} + \frac{K_B}{k}P_B + \frac{K_T}{k}P_T\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad Y_j = a_0 + a_1X_{1j} + a_2X_{2j}$$

Nonlinear regression (Example 10-1) minimises \(\sum(r_{obs}-r_{calc})^2\) directly and is preferred, because linearising distorts the error weights.

13Self-test

Derive the rate law for \(A \rightleftharpoons B\) (single site) if adsorption of A is rate-limiting and B is adsorbed.

\(r_{AD} = k_A(P_AC_v - C_{A\cdot S}/K_A)\). Surface reaction equilibrium: \(C_{A\cdot S} = C_{B\cdot S}/K_S\). Desorption equilibrium: \(C_{B\cdot S} = K_BP_BC_v\). So \(C_{A\cdot S} = K_BP_BC_v/K_S\) and

$$r_{AD} = k_AC_v\left(P_A - \frac{K_BP_B}{K_SK_A}\right) = k_AC_v\left(P_A - \frac{P_B}{K_P}\right),\quad K_P = \frac{K_SK_A}{K_B}$$ $$C_t = C_v\left(1 + \frac{K_BP_B}{K_S} + K_BP_B\right)\ \Rightarrow\ -r'_A = \frac{k_AC_t(P_A - P_B/K_P)}{1 + (K_B/K_S + K_B)P_B}$$

Note A is absent from the denominator: when adsorption of A is limiting, \(\mathrm{A\cdot S}\) is not in equilibrium with gas A.

A rate law is \(-r'_A = \dfrac{kP_A}{(1 + \sqrt{K_AP_A} + K_BP_B)^2}\). What does each feature say?

\(\sqrt{K_AP_A}\): A adsorbs dissociatively. Squared denominator: the limiting step involves two sites (e.g. the two A fragments reacting on adjacent sites). \(K_BP_B\): product B adsorbs molecularly and inhibits.

Why does the initial rate become independent of cumene pressure at high pressure if the surface reaction limits?

At high \(P_{C0}\), \(K_CP_{C0}\gg1\) and almost every site is covered by cumene (\(C_{C\cdot S}\to C_t\)). The rate \(k_SC_{C\cdot S}\) cannot grow any more: \(-r'_{C0}\to k/K_C = k_SC_t\).

In the toluene data, how do you know hydrogen is not adsorbed but toluene is?

Rate doubles each time \(P_{H_2}\) doubles (runs 7–9): first order at all pressures, so no saturation, so negligible coverage. Toluene shows saturation (runs 12–15 nearly flat), the signature of adsorption in the denominator.

What is PSSH and when would you use it instead of the rate-limiting-step method?

The net rate of formation of each adsorbed intermediate is set to zero, \(r^*_{i\cdot S}=0\). Use it when no single step is much slower than the others (two or more comparable steps).

At high temperature, why do catalytic rate laws often reduce to power laws?

Adsorption is exothermic, so adsorption constants fall with \(T\). Coverages become small, \(\sum K_iP_i\ll1\), the denominator tends to 1, and \(-r'_A\approx kP_A\).

Show that for any limiting step in cumene decomposition the rate vanishes at equilibrium.

Each form contains \(P_C - P_BP_P/K_P\) with \(K_P = K_CK_S/K_B = (P_BP_P/P_C)_{eq}\), which is zero at equilibrium. It comes from combining the quasi-equilibrium relations of the fast steps with the driving force of the slow step.

Fogler P10-5: \(-r'_E = \dfrac{kP_EP_H}{1 + K_EP_E}\) for ethylene hydrogenation. Suggest a mechanism.

\(E + S \rightleftharpoons E\cdot S\) (equilibrium); \(E\cdot S + H_2(g) \rightarrow A\cdot S\) (Eley–Rideal, limiting); \(A\cdot S \rightleftharpoons A + S\) with ethane weakly adsorbed. Then \(-r'_E = k_SC_{E\cdot S}P_H\), \(C_{E\cdot S} = K_EP_EC_t/(1+K_EP_E)\), which gives the rate law with \(k = k_SK_EC_t\). Hydrogen reacting from the gas explains why it is in the numerator but not the denominator.

In Fogler Example 10-2, why does the benzene partial pressure pass through a maximum along the packed bed, and what does the pressure drop cost?

\(P_B = y_BP\). Early in the bed benzene forms quickly, so \(y_B\) rises faster than \(P\) falls. Towards the end almost no benzene forms, \(y_B\) levels off, and the falling total pressure pulls \(P_B\) down. Pressure drop lowers the conversion of the 10 000 kg bed from 0.79 to 0.682, and the bed cannot exceed 10 197 kg or the exit pressure would fall below 1 atm.
