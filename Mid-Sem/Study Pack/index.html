What the professor said about Module 3

You do not need to focus on solving the differential equation for the concentration profile inside the porous catalyst. You do need the formulation of the problem (shell balance, flux law, rate law, boundary conditions) and the concepts of the effectiveness factor η\etaη and the Thiele modulus ϕ\phiϕ. The M3 page is built around that: formulation is done line by line, solutions are shown but marked "for understanding".

4A study plan that works

If you have three days

  1. Day 1: M1 page end to end. Redo Tutorial 1 Q2–Q6 on paper without looking.
  2. Day 2: M2 page. Derive the cumene rate law for all three limiting steps from a blank sheet. Then Tutorial 2 Q1 and Tutorial 3 Q1–Q2, Q6.
  3. Day 3: M3 page. Write the slab and sphere formulations from memory. Practice problems 9–15. Then the mock paper, timed.

If you have one evening

  1. Formula sheet, read twice.
  2. The "LHHW algorithm" box on M2 and the "formulation recipe" box on M3.
  3. The "Exam traps" section of the practice page.
  4. Practice problems 1, 2, 7, 9, 10 and 11 — they cover the most likely numericals.

6In the exam hall

Derivation questions

Examiners mark each stage, so always write them in this order:

  1. The mechanism, every step as an elementary reaction.
  2. The rate of the limiting step.
  3. Quasi-equilibrium relations for the other steps.
  4. The site balance.
  5. The final rate law.
  6. One line on what its form tells you.

The same template is drawn as a flowchart on the formula page.

Numerical questions

Write the formula symbolically first, then the numbers with units. State the regime you land in (for example "MT=2.0M_T = 2.0MT​=2.0, intermediate, so neither limiting form applies").
