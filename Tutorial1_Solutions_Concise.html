(ii) Synthesis and characterisation

Synthesis — fusion (ammonia Fe), precipitation/co-precipitation (Cu–ZnO methanol, Fe–Cr shift), impregnation to incipient wetness (Co–Mo/Al2O3, Ni/Al2O3, Pt/Al2O3), ion exchange and hydrothermal crystallisation (zeolite Y, ZSM-5), spray drying (FCC microspheres), followed by forming — extrusion, tabletting, oil-drop, washcoating — then drying, calcination and reduction or sulfidation.

Characterisation — N2 physisorption/BET for surface area; He–Hg displacement for pore volume, densities and porosity; Hg porosimetry and BJH for pore-size distribution; selective chemisorption for active-metal area and dispersion; XRD for phases and crystallite size; SEM–EDX and TEM for morphology; XPS for surface oxidation state; TPR/TPD/TPO for reducibility, acidity and coke; crush strength and attrition index for mechanical integrity.

Answer

1. Shape is set by the reactor: microspheres → fluidised/riser; lobed extrudates → trickle bed; rings and multi-hole cylinders → fired tubes and low-ΔP adiabatic beds; monolith → zero-ΔP exhaust; gauze → millisecond contact; powder → slurry.

2. Synthesis is fusion, precipitation, impregnation, ion exchange or hydrothermal, then forming; characterisation splits into texture (BET, He–Hg, porosimetry), chemistry (XRD, XPS, TPR/TPD) and function (chemisorption, isotherms).

3. In India the suppliers are Clariant/Süd-Chemie (Cochin, Vadodara), Johnson Matthey (Chennai, Taloja), BASF (Dahej, Chennai), Umicore (Pune), with Topsoe, UOP, Axens and Shell as licensors and IOCL R&D as the indigenous developer.

Q2

External surface area per unit volume of a 100 µm spherical Zeolite Y FCC catalyst, and the size needed for 320 m2/g. ρp = 1.5 g/cm3.

2 marks

Mark map — 1 for Sv, 1 for the required particle size.

For a sphere, external area per unit volume and per unit mass are

Sv= πdp2πdp3/6 =6dp Sg=Svρp =6ρpdp

(a) dp = 100 µm = 1×10−4 m = 1×10−2 cm:

Sv=61×10−4 =6×104 m2/m3 ⇒ Sg=6001.5=400 cm2/g=0.04 m2/g

(b) For Sg = 320 m2/g = 3.2×106 cm2/g:

dp=6ρpSg =61.5×3.2×106 =1.25×10−6 cm
Answer

(a) Sv = 6×104 m2/m3, i.e. only 0.04 m2/g.  (b) dp = 1.25×10−6 cm = 12.5 nm (radius 6.25×10−7 cm).

Such particles cannot be made or fluidised commercially — hence the 200–300 m2/g of an FCC catalyst must come from internal porosity, not external area.
Key convention: the printed answers (3×104 m2/m3, 6.25×10−7 cm) take 100 µm as a radius and quote a radius; they are the same physics, halved.

Q4

From the BET plot, estimate the surface area per gram of silica gel for (a) N2 at −195.8 °C and (b) O2 at −183 °C.

6 marks

Mark map — 1 BET equation and what its slope/intercept mean; 1 reading i and s off the plot; 1 monolayer volume vm; 1 the area equation and α; 1 Sg for N2; 1 Sg for O2.

Step 1 — BET equation. Langmuir extended to multilayers:

pv(p0−p) =1vmC +(C−1)pCvmp0 ⇒ vm=1i+s ,C=1+si

Step 2 — read the line. The ordinate axis starts at 1.0, so the intercept must be obtained by extrapolating to p/p0 = 0, not read off the axis.

Step 3 — monolayer volume.

vm= 1(1.0+20)×10−3 =47.6 cm3(STP)/g (C=21)

Step 4 — area equation.

Sg= vmN0αV (N0=6.02×1023,V=22400 cm3/mol)

α = 1.09[M/(N0ρ)]2/3 gives 16.2×10−16 cm2 for N2 (ρ = 0.808) and 14.2×10−16 cm2 for O2 (ρ = 1.14) — as given

Step 5 — (a) nitrogen.

Sg= 47.6×6.02×1023×16.2×10−1622400 =2.07×106 cm2/g

= 207 m2/g  (shortcut: Sg = 4.35 vm)

Step 6 — (b) oxygen. Only α changes, so Sg = 3.82×104 vm. Reading the O2 line the same way gives vm ≈ 53.5 cm3/g:

Sg=3.82×104×53.5 =2.04×106 cm2/g
Answer

(a) vm = 47.6 cm3/g → Sg ≈ 207–210 m2/g  ·  (b) Sg ≈ 204 m2/g. Both lie in the 200–600 m2/g band quoted for silica gels.

The 1–2 % spread against the key (209.65) is intercept-extrapolation error — quote your i and s. Part (b) needs the O2 line read separately: keeping vm = 47.6 and changing only α gives 182 m2/g. The gases disagree because Eq. (8-14) assumes bulk-liquid packing — which is why N2 at −195.8 °C is the accepted standard.

Q5

JM Smith Example 8-4 — pore volume, solid density and porosity of activated silica by helium–mercury displacement.

3 marks

Mark map — 1 for Vg, 1 for ρs, 1 for εp.

m = 101.5 g  ·  VHe = 45.1 cm3  ·  VHg = 82.7 cm3

Helium enters the pores, so VHe is the solid skeleton volume; mercury does not enter at 1 atm, so VHg is the envelope volume. The difference is the pore volume.

Vg= 82.7−45.1101.5 =0.371 cm3/g
ρs=101.545.1=2.25 g/cm3 ρp=101.582.7=1.23 g/cm3
εp= VgρsVgρs+1 =0.371×2.250.371×2.25+1 =0.8341.834=0.455

check: εp = Vgρp = 0.371 × 1.23 = 0.455 ✓

Answer

Vg = 0.371 cm3/g  ·  ρs = 2.25 g/cm3  ·  ρp = 1.23 g/cm3  ·  εp = 0.455

εp ≈ 0.5 is the expected value — with a bed voidage of 0.4 this gives the standard fixed-bed split of ~30 % pore, ~30 % solid, ~40 % interparticle void.

Q6

JM Smith Example 8-5 — bidisperse Ni/Al2O3 pellet: densities, void fractions and solid fraction.

4 marks

Mark map — 8 parts, ½ mark each. Watch the basis: macropore volume is per pellet, micropore volume is per gram of particles.

m = 3.15 g  ·  Vpellet = 3.22 cm3  ·  macropore volume = 0.645 cm3 per pellet  ·  micropore volume = 0.40 cm3/g of particles

Micropores lie inside the alumina particles (they carry the surface area); macropores are the voids between particles, created by pelleting. The closure relation is εM + εμ + εs = 1.

The pellet is 0.591 void and 0.409 solid; the particles alone are 0.49 void. Do not confuse (d) with (h) — both are micropore porosity, but (d) is per pellet and (h) is per particle.
