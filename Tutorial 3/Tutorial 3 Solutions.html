How to read these solutions

We use S for a vacant catalytic site, and a dot before S for an occupied site, for example A·S. A surface concentration such as CA·S counts occupied sites; a gas concentration or pressure is written CA or pA. An adsorption equilibrium constant K has units that make Kp dimensionless.

Core algorithm. (1) Choose the rate-limiting step. (2) Put the rate in terms of surface coverages. (3) Use quasi-equilibrium adsorption steps to replace coverages by gas pressures and vacant sites. (4) Use a site balance to eliminate vacant sites. This is Langmuir-Hinshelwood (LH: a model where reacting molecules are both adsorbed) analysis.

For every reversible elementary step, the net rate is forward minus reverse. For an irreversible step, only the forward term remains. The derivations below state the assumptions explicitly; changing the rate-limiting step changes the denominator and sometimes the pressure dependence.

Question 1 - three limiting cases

(i) Single-site reaction: A·S → B·S

Assume A and B adsorb molecularly and adsorption is at equilibrium:

CA·S = KApACv,   CB·S = KBpBCv

The single-site balance is Ct=Cv+CA·S+CB·S. Therefore Cv=Ct/(1+KApA+KBpB). If the surface reaction is irreversible and rate limiting, -r′A=ksCA·S. Absorb Ct into k=ksCt:

−r′A = k pA / (1+KApA+KBpB)

(ii) Dual-site reaction: A·S + S → B·S + S

The same adsorption relations apply, but A·S consumes one occupied site and one additional vacant site in the rate-limiting event. Thus -r′A=ksCA·SCv. Substitution gives:

−r′A = k pA / (1+KApA+KBpB)2

The square is the signature of needing two vacant-site factors.

(iii) Dual-site reaction: A·S + B·S → C·S + S

Here the rate is ksCA·SCB·S. Substituting both coverages and the site balance gives:

−r′A = k pApB / (1+KApA+KBpB+KCpC)2

(iv) Eley-Rideal reaction

Eley-Rideal (ER: a gas molecule reacts directly with an adsorbed molecule) uses A·S+B(g)→C·S. The rate is ksCA·SpB. With the single-site balance:

−r′A = k pApB / (1+KApA+KCpC)

Question 2 - n-pentene isomerisation

Let N be n-pentene, I be i-pentene, and Kp=KNKS/KI. Adsorption equilibrium gives CN·S=KNpNCv and desorption equilibrium gives CI·S=(KIpI/KS)Cv. A site balance gives Cv=Ct/(1+KNpN+KIpI).

(i) N·S + S ⇌ I·S + S

The reversible dual-site rate is ks(CN·SCv−CI·SCv/Ks). Substituting and using Kp gives:

−r′N= k(pN−pI/Kp)/(1+KNpN+KIpI)2

(ii) N·S ⇌ I·S

Now the rate-limiting step needs only one occupied site, so -r′N=ks(CN·S−CI·S/Ks). The same substitutions produce:

−r′N= k(pN−pI/Kp)/(1+KNpN+KIpI)
Sign check. If pN>pI/Kp, N disappears. At equilibrium the bracket is zero, as it must be.

Question 3 - Fogler P10-3: TBA production

Use I=isobutene, W=water, and T=TBA. The adsorption steps are I+S⇌I·S and W+S⇌W·S; T·S⇌T+S is desorption. Let Kc be the overall equilibrium constant and Ct be the site concentration.

(a) Surface reaction limiting

For I·S+W·S⇌T·S+S, the reversible rate is ks(CI·SCW·S−CT·SCv/Ks). With T desorption equilibrium and the site balance, the standard result is:

r′T= k(CICW−CT/Kc)/[(1+KICI)(1+KWCW)]

(b) Isobutene adsorption limiting

The net adsorption rate is r′=kI(CICv−CI·S/KI). All later steps are at equilibrium, so their equilibrium relations connect the net I uptake to net TBA formation. The resulting form is:

r′T= k(CI−CT/(KcCW))/(1+KWCW)

(c) ER kinetics, surface reaction limiting

For I·S+W(g)→T·S+S, the gas water is not in the site balance. With I adsorption at equilibrium:

r′T= k CICW/(1+KICI)

(d) I and W use different site families

With independent balances Ct1=Cv1+CI·S1 and Ct2=Cv2+CW·S2:

r′T= k CICW/[(1+KICI)(1+KWCW)]

(The constants can absorb Ct1Ct2.)

(e) Generalisation

The same overall reaction can produce different empirical rate laws because the rate-limiting step and site arrangement determine which coverages appear in the numerator and denominator. Therefore a rate law alone is evidence for a mechanism, not a unique proof of one.

Question 4 - Fogler P10-5: ethylene hydrogenation

Given −r′E=k pEpH/(1+KEpE), propose molecular hydrogen adsorption E+S⇌E·S, weak or non-rate-limiting hydrogen adsorption, and a surface reaction E·S+H(g)→A·S. If the surface reaction is limiting, its rate is ksCE·SpH. Since CE·S=KEpECv and Cv=Ct/(1+KEpE), absorbing KECt into k gives exactly:

−r′E=k pEpH/(1+KEpE)

The difficult part is recognizing that the absence of a hydrogen adsorption term in the denominator does not mean hydrogen is absent; it means hydrogen is weakly adsorbed or its coverage is not controlling the site balance.

Question 5 - Fogler P10-6: propanol formation

Mechanism: O2+2S⇌2O·S; C3H6+O·S→C3H5OH·S; and C3H5OH·S⇌C3H5OH+S.

Choose the middle surface reaction as rate-limiting. Dissociative oxygen adsorption gives CO·S=√(KOpO2)Cv. Propylene reacts directly from the gas phase, so it has no adsorption term. If product desorption is fast, the product coverage is negligible and the site balance is Ct=Cv+CO·S. Hence:

Cv=Ct/(1+√(KOpO2))
−r′C3H6=k pC3H6√(KOpO2)/(1+√(KOpO2))

The square root comes from dissociative adsorption: one O2 molecule occupies two sites and creates two O·S species.

Question 6 - Toluene hydrogenation: data analysis and regression

Reaction: T+H2→B+M. The table heading says the measured rates are in units of 10−10 gmol T/(gcat·s). The regression model is:

−r′T= k pTpH / (1+KBpB+KTpT)
Important notation. pH is the partial pressure of H2. It is not pH2. Runs 7–9 show first-order dependence on H2: 71.8→142→284 when pH2 is 1→2→4 atm.

(ii) Mechanism consistent with the model

Use molecular adsorption of T and B, weak adsorption of methane, and a surface reaction involving adsorbed T and hydrogen:

T+S⇌T·S; B+S⇌B·S; H2+S⇌H2·S (weak); T·S+H2(g)→B·S+M(g) (slow).

The site balance is Ct=Cv+CT·S+CB·S. Therefore Cv=Ct/(1+KTpT+KBpB). The slow-step rate is proportional to CT·SpH2, producing the model.

Checks and common mistakes

Site-balance check
Every denominator comes from the species included in the site balance. Do not add a product term unless the product adsorbs.
Reaction-order check
For dissociative adsorption, coverage varies with √p. For a dual-site slow step, two coverage factors create a squared denominator.
Units check
In Q6 the table is scaled by 10−10; the fitted rate constant must be rescaled when reporting physical rates.
Notebook check
Run all cells from top to bottom. The final table should show predicted rates close to observed rates and R² close to 1.
Bug journal - likely pitfall. If Q6 is coded as pH22, runs 7–9 cannot fit: doubling pH2 would predict four times the rate, but the data show twice. This is a notation-reading error, not a regression failure.

Glossary

TermMeaning
AdsorptionAttachment of a gas or liquid molecule to a catalyst surface.
CoverageFraction or concentration of sites occupied by a species.
Rate-limiting stepThe slow step that controls the overall reaction rate.
Langmuir-HinshelwoodBoth reacting molecules are adsorbed before the surface reaction.
Eley-RidealA gas-phase molecule reacts directly with an adsorbed species.
Nonlinear regressionNumerically choosing model parameters that minimize the difference between measured and predicted data.
